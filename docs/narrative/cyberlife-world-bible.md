# Cyberlife — World Bible & Narrative Setting Guide

## 1. Setting Overview & Period

**Cyberlife** is set in a grounded, near-future (circa 2026–2027) interpretation of **Lagos Island, Nigeria**—a hyper-dense, historic, and rapidly transforming urban environment where centuries of maritime trade, colonial-era civic administration, legacy commercial banking, and bleeding-edge digital infrastructure intersect.

Lagos Island is neither a neon-soaked dystopian caricature nor a generic high-tech backdrop. It is an authentic, pulsating African megacity operating under real-world economic, infrastructural, and social dynamics:
- **Broad Street and Marina** form the historical and contemporary financial heartbeat of West Africa, housing commercial bank headquarters, maritime customs clearinghouses, and fiber-optic landing station interconnects.
- **Balogun Market** wraps around corporate high-rises, creating a vibrant grid of merchant commerce, informal electronics refurbishment, and mobile money agency kiosks.
- **Tinubu Square** stands as the historic civic nexus, where retired civil servants, legal practitioners, and civic activists debate public policy under the shadow of the central fountain.
- **The Marina Seawall and CMS Transit Hub** channel hundreds of thousands of daily commuters via Bus Rapid Transit (BRT), yellow Danfo minibuses, water taxis from Ikorodu and Victoria Island, and pedestrian boardwalks.

### Technology Level & Societal Infrastructure

In 2026, the technology landscape has arrived at a critical inflection point:
1. **The Autonomous Agent Boom**: Nigerian commercial banks, fintech unicorns, and logistics conglomerates have aggressively deployed enterprise autonomous AI agents for customer onboarding, loan adjudication, fraud monitoring, and automated IT operations.
2. **Infrastructure Fragility**: Despite submarine cable landings (Equiano, 2Africa) bringing massive bandwidth to the coastline, local power reliability remains tied to diesel generator schedules, solar inverters, and unstable municipal grids. Every server room, cybercafé, and apartment workspace hums with the acoustic texture of generators.
3. **The Dual Economy**: Million-dollar cloud contracts and fintech venture capital exist side-by-side with cash transactions, POS terminal operators, and informal street credit. A junior cybersecurity analyst earning their first consulting bounty must still calculate bus fare, generator fuel costs, and quarterly apartment rent.

## 2. Core Themes

### 2.1 Digital Sovereignty vs. Technological Dependency
West African enterprises operate under intense pressure to modernize by adopting foreign cloud platforms, proprietary AI frameworks, and international compliance checklists. When an autonomous system malfunctions or leaks data, foreign vendors point to local configuration errors, while local executives scramble to preserve their international partnerships. The struggle for true digital sovereignty—understanding, inspecting, and controlling one's own systems—drives the game's conflicts.

### 2.2 The Human Cost of Corporate Speed
Modern security failures rarely stem from evil geniuses in dark basements. They occur because overworked junior administrators face impossible executive deadlines, business stakeholders bypass security gates to beat competitors to market, and organizations treat human workers as expendable scapegoats when architectural shortcuts blow up.

### 2.3 The Physical-Digital Convergence
Cybersecurity in Lagos is never confined to an abstract terminal. Access control badges are copied in bustling market stalls; rogue hardware drops happen on crowded transit buses; air-gapped forensic labs sit behind unassuming storefronts; and an incident responder must physically navigate street checkpoints, security turnstiles, and local relationships to uncover the ground truth.

### 2.4 Integrity, Whistleblowing, and Survival
Players face continuous ethical choices:
- Adhere to strict corporate procedure and protect company liability?
- Protect a vulnerable junior engineer from being scapegoated by leadership?
- Whistleblow to investigative journalists or regulators when systemic misconduct is uncovered?
- Or operate as a pragmatic independent researcher claiming bug bounties to pay rent and upgrade their hardware?

## 4. Fictional Technology Ecosystem

All major institutions and technological infrastructure in Cyberlife are original, fictional creations tailored to this setting:

### Fictional Corporate & Financial Entities
- **Cedarline Services Ltd.**: An established West African enterprise cloud services and logistics software provider headquartered at Marina Tower. Rushed their proprietary customer assistant ("Aster") to market ahead of a critical international audit.
- **Eko Merchant Bank (EMB)**: A conservative, tier-1 commercial bank operating from Broad Street. Relies heavily on Cedarline’s data pipelines while maintaining extreme institutional secrecy regarding loan risk and transaction auditing.
- **Krest Incident Response & Data Solutions**: A high-end private cybersecurity and digital forensics firm. Sleek, expensive, and ruthless with Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs); often hired by corporate executives to perform "quiet cleanups" that minimize public exposure.

### Fictional Research & Civil Society Collectives
- **0xLagos Research Syndicate**: An informal collective of ethical hackers, bug bounty researchers, and reverse engineers who meet along the Marina seawall, local tech hubs, and encrypted forums. They believe in radical transparency and defensive proof-of-work.
- **The Lagos Inquirer (The Wire & Watchdog)**: An independent, public-interest investigative journalism bureau operating near Tinubu Square. Known for unmasking corporate fraud, digital surveillance, and public infrastructure decay.
- **Civic Rights & Cyber Integrity Initiative (CRCII)**: A digital rights NGO focused on consumer privacy, algorithmic accountability, and defending citizens against unlawful data collection and mobile payment fraud.
- **Balogun Traders Electronic Union**: The merchant association led by Mama Nkechi that coordinates security, fraud alerts, and collective purchasing for hundreds of local electronics vendors and POS agents.

### Fictional Regulatory & Public Institutions
- **National Digital Security Regulatory Commission (NDSRC)**: The statutory body responsible for enforcing national cybersecurity frameworks, breach notifications, and critical infrastructure protection. Stretched thin, politically pressured, yet home to dedicated public-interest inspectors.

## 5. Technology Rules & Cyber Operations in Cyberlife

To ensure absolute educational value, technical realism, and safety, Cyberlife enforces strict simulation rules:

1. **Deterministic In-Memory Reality**: All network hosts (`*.cedarline.internal`, `*.sim.net`), IP subnets (`10.0.4.0/24`), routing tables, SIEM logs, API endpoints, and user credentials exist strictly as in-memory domain models.
2. **Zero Host Side-Effects**: The game’s terminal and tools never call the player’s actual operating system (`child_process.exec`, `spawn`, `eval`), never open raw network sockets, and never access real files.
3. **No Weaponized Payloads**: Exploits and attack vectors are represented through conceptual logic, diagnostic traces, protocol specifications (e.g. SMB2 tree connect anomalies, BOLA/IDOR CWE-639, over-privileged IAM wildcards), and defensive validation rather than actionable malicious code.
4. **Forensic Integrity Over "Magic Hacking"**: Investigations advance by collecting verifiable digital evidence (cryptographic SHA-256 hashes, correlated timestamped logs, network PCAP traces, access control lists) and corroborating them with human testimony.

## 6. Tone, Style & Continuity Principles

- **Tone**: Grounded, intelligent, tense, empathetic, and culturally rich. Humor emerges organically from Lagos street life and bureaucratic absurdities—not from cartoonish tropes.
- **Visual & Narrative Aesthetics**: Premium, authentic, and detailed. Textures incorporate polished teak desks in executive offices, dust-streaked monitors in server closets, colorful Dutch wax ankara prints in street markets, and humid coastal evening lighting across the Lagos lagoon.
- **Dialogue Authenticity**: Characters speak in natural Nigerian English, seasoned with authentic Lagos expressions, corporate tech jargon, or colorful Pidgin where contextually appropriate, reflecting their background and social standing.
- **Continuity Rules**:
  - *Hard Evidence vs. Speculation*: A character’s claim is not treated as fact until verified by log traces or physical documentation.
  - *No Omniscient NPCs*: Characters only know what their position, clearance, and daily routine would reasonably expose them to.
  - *Consequential Persistence*: Actions have memory. Blaming a colleague, leaking a document, or helping an NPC permanently updates world flags, relationships, and faction standings.
